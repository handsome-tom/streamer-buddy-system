Tester agreement

Version 1, November 15, 2026. For anyone testing StreamSpace or Streamer Buddy System before it's released.

Short version: you're getting an early look at something we made. Use it, break it, tell us what you think. Don't copy it, share the files, or build your own version from it. We'll be straight with you, and we trust you to do the same. We're all doing this in good faith.

What this covers

This agreement is between you and the people who make StreamSpace ("we", "us"). It covers the tester copy of the StreamSpace Chrome extension, the test site at test.streamspace.tv, Streamer Buddy System, and anything else we share with you as a tester, like early builds, screenshots, guides, plans and ideas (together, "the project"). Our regular terms of use and privacy policy still apply. If they disagree with this agreement, this one wins for testing.

It's ours

The project belongs to us: the code, the design and how it looks, the names, logos and pictures, the features and how they work, and the writing. Being a tester doesn't give you any ownership of it. We give you a personal, free permission to install and use the project while you're a tester. You can't pass that permission on to anyone else, and we can end it at any time.

What you agree not to do

  • Copy, share, upload, sell or give away the project's files, or the download links we send you, to anyone who isn't a tester.
  • Take apart the project to copy it, or use its code, design, features or look to make your own extension, app, site or service, or to help someone else make one.
  • Pass off the project, or any part of it, as your own.
  • Share unreleased features, plans or private details publicly (for example in videos, posts or other servers) without asking us first.
  • Use the project to break Twitch's rules, or to harass, spam or harm anyone.
  • Attack, overload or try to get around the limits of our relay, sign-in or test site, or get into anyone else's account or data.

Using StreamSpace on your own stream, the normal way, is fine. That's what it's for. When you're not sure whether something is OK, just ask.

Bugs and security problems

If you find a bug, tell us. If you find a security problem, like a way to see someone else's messages or get in where you shouldn't, tell us privately and don't use it or share it. Finding problems is the point of testing, and we're grateful for it.

Your feedback

Ideas, suggestions and bug reports you send us are freely given. We can use them to improve the project without paying you or owing you anything. If an idea of yours ends up in StreamSpace, that's a win for everyone, and we'll happily say thank you.

Test versions break

Test versions are unfinished. They can have bugs, lose settings or data, stop working, or change without warning. They're provided "as is", without any warranty, and the "No warranty" and "Limit of liability" parts of our terms of use apply. Keep backups of anything you care about.

Good faith, both ways

We're a small project made by people who love Twitch. We'll be honest with you about what the project does, we won't collect more about you than it needs, and we'll listen to what you tell us. In return, we're trusting you with something we haven't released yet. We're all going about this in good faith.

What's recorded when you agree

When you agree, we keep the Twitch name you typed, the time, which version of this agreement it was, where you agreed (the extension or the test site), the build you were using, and the country your connection came from. That's so we have a record of who agreed. Nothing else about you is sent.

Ending it

You can stop testing at any time: remove the extension and delete any files we sent you. We can end your tester access at any time, especially if this agreement is broken. "It's ours" and "What you agree not to do" still apply after testing ends.

Changes

If this agreement changes, you'll be asked to agree to the new version before you keep testing.
